# Natural Language Verification

Natural language verification is the workflow of describing a strategy
in plain English, having Honba generate the implementation, run a
backtest, and produce a critique in plain English. It is useful for
two purposes: writing strategies quickly, and getting a second opinion
on a strategy you wrote yourself.

## Two Workflows

### Workflow 1: NL → Strategy

You describe what you want. Honba writes it, backtests it, and tells
you whether it works.

    from honba.ai.verification import nl_to_strategy, critique

    result = nl_to_strategy(
        description="""
        Buy the top 5 stocks in NIFTY 50 by 3-month momentum
        at the start of each month, hold until the next month,
        with a 5% trailing stop loss.
        """,
        universe="nifty50",
        start="2020-01-01",
        end="2024-12-31",
    )

    print(result.strategy_code)
    print(result.backtest.summary())

    print(critique(result))
    # The strategy has a Sharpe of 1.12 and a CAGR of 17.4%.
    # Costs consume 4.2% of capital annually, dominated by STT
    # (delivery, 0.1% per side) and rebalancing turnover. The
    # monthly rebalance frequency produces 60 trades per year,
    # which is high for a 5-position portfolio. Reducing to
    # quarterly would roughly halve costs.
    #
    # The strategy is concentrated: the top 5 positions hold
    # 100% of capital. If any single position gaps down, the
    # portfolio bears the full impact. Consider 10 positions.
    #
    # The trailing stop triggers 14 times over the backtest,
    # mostly in high-volatility months. Its net contribution is
    # negative: excluding stopped trades, the Sharpe rises to
    # 1.28. The stop is cutting winners more often than losers.

The critique identifies concrete issues. It is generated by the LLM
reading the backtest result, the strategy code, and the cost
breakdown.

### Workflow 2: Critique an Existing Strategy

You wrote a strategy. You want an independent review.

    from honba.ai.verification import critique

    report = critique(
        strategy=SmaCrossover,     # a class
        result=backtest_result,
        depth="thorough",          # "quick", "standard", "thorough"
    )

    report.print()
    report.save_markdown("critique.md")

## What the Critique Covers

A thorough critique examines six dimensions:

### 1. Cost Sensitivity

    Cost contribution:      4.2% of capital annually
    Dominant component:     STT (delivery, 0.1% per side)
    Break-even gross edge:  4.2% (unusually high)
    Turnover:               60 trades/year (high)

    The strategy's edge is concentrated in a small number of trades.
    Removing costs, Sharpe rises to 1.51; with costs, 1.12. A 25%
    increase in trading frequency would eliminate the edge.

### 2. Concentration and Diversification

    Number of positions:    5
    Max single position:    22% of capital (above 15% guideline)
    Sector concentration:   Financials 48% (above 35% guideline)
    Largest holding:        HDFCBANK (22%)

    The portfolio is not diversified. A 10% drawdown in HDFCBANK
    alone would produce a 2.2% portfolio loss.

### 3. Robustness

    OOS/IS Sharpe ratio:    0.71 (acceptable)
    Walk-forward folds:     6/8 profitable
    MC 5th percentile:      0.68
    Parameter plateau:      0.42 (marginal)

    The parameter surface has a ridge rather than a plateau. Small
    changes to the momentum lookback (90 → 100 days) reduce Sharpe
    to 0.82. This is a fragile parameterization.

### 4. Regime Dependence

    Regime              Sharpe
    ────────────────────────────
    Bull (low vol)      1.62
    Bull (high vol)     0.94
    Bear (low vol)      -0.18
    Bear (high vol)     0.42
    Choppy              1.12

    The strategy loses money in bear markets with low volatility.
    This is a common pattern for momentum strategies; the
    performance in bear (high vol) is better because high
    volatility creates more dispersion in momentum scores.

### 5. Trade Distribution

    Total trades:       342
    Avg trade P&L:      ₹1,240
    Median trade P&L:   ₹680
    Top 5 trades:       42% of total P&L (high)

    A disproportionate share of P&L comes from a small number of
    trades. Removing the top 5 trades reduces Sharpe to 0.71. This
    suggests the strategy has a small edge on most trades and a
    large edge on a few — the latter is likely luck.

### 6. Implementation Concerns

    - The trailing stop uses `bar.low` for triggering but `bar.close`
      for exit price. In real trading, the stop would trigger at the
      low and fill at the next available price, which could be worse.

    - The strategy rebalances on the first trading day of the month.
      Since the strategy holds no positions on the first day, the
      rebalance happens at the open, which is the least liquid time
      of day.

    - `self.position(instr).is_long` is checked without verifying the
      position size. If a partial fill occurs, the strategy might
      hold less than intended.

## How the Critique Is Generated

The critique is produced by an LLM with access to:

- The strategy source code.
- The backtest result (trades, equity curve, statistics).
- The validation report (all six tests).
- The cost breakdown.
- The regime analysis.
- The instrument metadata (for concentration analysis).

The LLM's prompt explicitly instructs it to:

- Identify concrete issues, not generic warnings.
- Reference specific numbers from the backtest.
- Distinguish between fatal issues and areas for improvement.
- Avoid recommending changes that would introduce look-ahead bias.

The critique is not a black box. The full prompt and the LLM's
reasoning are saved alongside the report.

## What the Critique Does Not Do

- **It does not guarantee correctness.** An LLM can miss real bugs and
  flag non-issues. The critique is a starting point for review, not a
  replacement for it.
- **It does not run additional backtests.** A thorough critique
  describes what to test next; running those tests is the user's
  responsibility (or the MCP agent's).
- **It does not know your constraints.** The critique assumes a
  generic retail investor with a ₹10 lakh portfolio and daily bars.
  If your situation is different, some of its judgments will not
  apply.

## The Critique Schema

    from honba.ai.verification import Critique

    class Critique(BaseModel):
        summary: str
        cost_analysis: str
        concentration_analysis: str
        robustness_analysis: str
        regime_analysis: str
        trade_analysis: str
        implementation_concerns: list[str]
        recommendations: list[str]
        severity: str  # "pass" | "review" | "reject"

Each section is markdown text. The `severity` field is a single
judgment: `pass` if the strategy is ready for paper trading,
`review` if there are concerns worth addressing, `reject` if there is
a fatal flaw.

## The Natural Language Strategy Description

The `nl_to_strategy` function accepts descriptions in natural
language. The description is parsed into a structured specification,
which is then converted to code.

The parser understands:

- **Universe:** "NIFTY 50", "Alpha 30", "top 100 by market cap"
- **Signal:** "momentum", "mean reversion", "RSI below 30"
- **Ranking:** "top 5 by 3-month return", "lowest volatility"
- **Position sizing:** "equal weight", "risk parity", "volatility scaled"
- **Frequency:** "monthly", "weekly", "daily"
- **Stops:** "5% trailing stop", "2-ATR stop"
- **Constraints:** "long only", "max 20 positions"

Ambiguity in the description produces a clarifying question rather
than a guess:

    result = nl_to_strategy(
        description="Buy stocks that are doing well.",
        ...
    )
    # → Clarification needed: "doing well" is ambiguous.
    #   Do you mean: (a) recent price momentum, (b) earnings growth,
    #   (c) relative strength vs the index, or (d) something else?

This is important. An LLM that guesses at ambiguous descriptions will
produce strategies that do not match the user's intent, and the user
will spend more time debugging than if they had written the code
themselves.

    # configs/ai/verification.toml
    [nl_to_strategy]
    llm_provider = "anthropic"
    model = "claude-sonnet-4-5"
    clarify_on_ambiguity = true
    max_clarifications = 3

    [critique]
    default_depth = "standard"
    include_code_review = true
    include_validation = true
    save_prompts = true
    output_dir = "./reports/critiques"

## When This Is Useful

- **Rapid prototyping.** Describe a strategy, get a first version,
  iterate. Faster than writing from scratch for straightforward ideas.
- **Second opinion.** You wrote a strategy; a critique catches issues
  you may have missed.
- **Education.** Reading critiques of strategies (yours or others')
  is one of the fastest ways to learn what makes a strategy robust.

## When This Is Not Useful

- **Complex strategies.** A description of a pairs-trading strategy
  with dynamic hedging will produce code with subtle bugs. Write it
  yourself.
- **Novel signals.** The LLM generates conventional strategies. If
  your idea is unusual, describe it in more detail or write the code
  and ask for a critique.
- **Anything requiring domain knowledge the LLM does not have.**
  Niche Indian market microstructure details are often not in the
  training data. The critique may be confidently wrong.

## A Note on Trust

The most dangerous failure mode of natural language verification is
that it produces *plausible* critiques that are wrong. An LLM can
describe a strategy as robust when it is overfit, or as overfit when
it is fine. It can recommend changes that make the strategy worse.

Use the critique as a checklist, not as an authority. Every point it
makes should be verifiable by inspecting the code or running an
additional test. If a critique is correct, the test will confirm it.
If it is wrong, the test will show that too.

The value of the critique is not that it is correct. It is that it
gives you a list of things to check that you might not have thought
of. That list is valuable even when some items on it are wrong.
